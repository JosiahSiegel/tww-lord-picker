Introduction
This guide will show you how to trigger all the lore events in the Legendary Lore mod for Total War: Warhammer III.

GUIDE LAST UPDATED: 1 JUNE 2026.

There are seven separate ways to trigger the lore events in this mod:

Taking a Legendary Lord to a region.

Defeating a Legendary Lord in combat.

Upgrading the level of a settlement.

Occupying or razing settlements.

Building a landmark or certain buildings.

Recruiting a unit or agent.

Reaching a certain turn.

How many events are in the mod? (05/05/2024)

329 versus events.

288 landmark events.

379 settlement events.

921 unit, agent and regiments of renown events.

452 occupation events.

1043 legendary lord (exploring) events.

273 generic lore events.
TOTAL = 3685

This guide is split by factions. Since nearly every unit and landmark triggers lore, they won't be included unless to note that they're still under construction. If you see the letters IME or RoC next to region names - that is to denote whether they're Immortal Empires or Realm of Chaos campaign.
Canon
The mod's goal is to ensure every Legendary Lord, Legendary Hero, unit, Regiment of Renown and settlement triggers lore pop ups, as well as providing many Legendary Lord v Legendary Lord events and occupying cultural capitals events.

The Warhammer Fantasy universe spans decades of rulebooks, novels, and White Dwarf magazines. Some Legendary Lords have entire trilogies dedicated to them; others have only a few paragraphs of official text to their name. Some Legendary Lords had climatic battles in the End Times, but the vast, vast majority never even encountered one another, let alone fought one another. Therefore, a substantial amount of original content is required to achieve the mod's goal.

I do not have any plans or interest to make a strictly 'canon only' mod or feature to separate or differentiate canon and original content. If you want to strictly read official lore then go to the official sources - not the Steam Workshop.

Original Content Writing Policy:
I aim to ensure all original content respects canon as much as possible. This is achieved with an additive, rather than a transformative, approach. Examples:


No major original characters eg Karl Franz's long-lost brother or a brand new Cathayan Dragon Child. However, minor, inconsequential characters (like a Cathayan local guide assisting Markus Wulfhart hunt Nakai) can be created purely to drive the narrative of that particular lore event.

No self-inserts like Oh_Man was the king of all the realms, or aliens came down and started probing everyone, or entire new continents, etc. No silliness - the canon is treated with respect.

Retcons - I try my best to harmonise retcons when I encounter them. I use techiques similar to those you would see from apologetics re: biblical inerrancy as I find that gets the best results. But, of course, once you take contradicting official source A and contradicting official source B and harmonise them with your original content source C it's no longer canon, is it?

Truncation - sometimes official material is way too large for a lore pop-up. So of course this involves transformation either splitting them then modifying openings and closings to each sub-story or radically truncating the story into a summary. Of course this means it's no longer canon, but neither is it original content, is it?

Favouring the video game over the board game - often I encounter situations of lore mismatches ie. Arnheim being intact in Total War but a ruin in the Dark Elf book. I always favour the game's lore over board game lore because this mod is for the game.

End Times - I treat End Times not as the canonical ending but only one of many possible futures in the game. If you are playing as Archaon, of course you make End Times happen. But if you play as Karl Franz, you are averting the End TImes. I do not treat it as an inevitable outcome for every character. However, I have "Vision of the End Times" events (triggers on turn 100 or go to the Chamber of Visions) which are used to show the player what happens to their character in the canonical End Times. If they weren't in the End Times, original content is written for the Vision event.

Typos/bad English - When I see them I fix them.

In conclusion, and as food for thought, I leave you with these quotes re canon from past and present GW employees:

"With Warhammer and 40k, the notion of canon is a fallacy. [...] Warhammer exists as tens of thousands of overlapping realities in the imaginations of game developers, writers, readers and gamers. None of those interpretations is wrong."
- Gave Thorpe, Lead Designer GW

"It all stems from the assumption that there's a binding contract between author and reader to adhere to some non-existent subjective construct or 'true' representation of the setting. There is no such contract, and no such objective truth."
- Andy Hoare, Game Designer GW

"There is no canon. There are several hundred creators all adding to the melting pot of the IP."
- Aaron Dembski-Bowden, Co-Author of the Horus Heresy series

"Here's our standard line: Yes, it's all official, but remember that we're reporting back from a time where stories aren't always true, or at least 100% accurate. If it has the 40k logo on it, it exists in the 40k universe. Or it was a legend that may well have happened. Or a rumour that may or may not have any truth behind it."
- Marc Gascogne, Chief Editor of the Black Library
